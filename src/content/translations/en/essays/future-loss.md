Rubbing my aching neck, I caught sight of my unruly hair reflected in the monitor. It seemed I was beginning, once again, to regret the path I had chosen.

To be honest, somewhere deep down I always seem on the verge of another bout of artistic pretension: unwilling to let my still-undiscovered talent go to waste, only to keep realizing, rather comically, how little I actually have to say.

There is a joke I often tell: people say that if you follow your dreams, you will never work a day in your life. Only the art students took it seriously—and sure enough, they ended up without jobs.

Back to me: having drifted through nearly two decades (?), I can proudly boast about all the hobbies I have given up. And if someone asks why? The usual answers are “too busy with school,” or “I knew I had no great talent”…

I am no longer ashamed to admit that I have always envied the grace of the arts. But whenever I think of taking up those old pursuits again, I brush the thought aside with the excuse that I must not let them get in the way of more important things.

What exactly am I regretting? That I never became a spirited musician, a philosopher who could see clearly through right and wrong, an artist with an unfettered imagination… That I am now just an overworked student with a sore neck, resenting the disheveled figure reflected in the screen.

Perhaps it is not only artists I envy. More precisely, it seems to be the silhouette of an idealist. I want to accomplish something, to have my thoughts expressed and perhaps even recognized. I want to set aside material constraints and pursue the person I am in my daydreams.

If I could, I really would choose again!  
…  
Would choosing again leave me without regrets?  
…  
I think I would still have them.

Life is a one-way road. Perhaps we have all regretted the things we will one day lose. When I have a little spare time, I should touch those dusty black and white keys more often. I do not want to keep stammering out the same pointless regrets.

Life seldom grants our wishes; tomorrow, I will let my hair down and drift away in a little boat.
