I have heard that our perception of the world is logarithmic. If so, in terms of how time feels, perhaps half my life has already passed.

My eighteenth year can hardly be called uneventful. There was the pressure in the month before the university entrance exam; the loneliness of switching off the lights in an empty dormitory; the joy of a graduation trip with friends; the novelty of arriving in Europe and beginning a different path. I visited quite a few cities, saw beautiful scenery, immersed myself in unfamiliar cultures, and met all sorts of people…

This year seems to have held more experiences than all three years of high school put together. Yet whenever I try to find fragments of those memories in my mind, I come back empty-handed. I started taking a camera out with me, and the photographs are sharp and clear, but the feeling of the memories is hopelessly blurred. My high-school photos are goofy and scattered, yet behind their poor image quality are vivid memories that still bring back a complicated mix of feelings.

I have also heard that a life needs a foundation, and that people tend to seek it in one of two places: meaning through responsibility, or memories through experience.

I do not think either direction is right or wrong. It is a personal choice, so long as it makes sense to the person living it. But looking back at my own year, I have not worked particularly hard at my studies, while my footsteps have crossed half the globe. All that pleasure and passing splendor, yet the memories feel like water without a source.

“Perhaps it really is time to put my feet more firmly on the ground.”

I suspect my teachers in Europe would tease me about my Chinese way of thinking if they read this. Still, I hope life’s meaning amounts to more than drinking, partying, and beach holidays. As a foundation for a life, sensory pleasures seem too insubstantial to bear much weight or leave truly precious memories.

As Pavel Korchagin puts it, a life should be lived so that, looking back, one feels neither anguish over wasted years nor shame at having done nothing.

Midnight rambling—take it as a joke.  
Back to the point: happy nineteenth birthday.

For this new year, I only hope to meet its challenges steadily and set out with a lighter load.
