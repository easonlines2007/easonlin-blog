Since it was short, I picked up *Goodbye, Eri* on a late-night whim and read it straight through. I had heard so much about it, and Tatsuki Fujimoto’s command of emotion in a short story really is impeccable. In just thirty minutes, the story pulled me back and forth until, once again, closing the screen left me with a bottomless emptiness.

Thinking back, I always seem to go through a kind of withdrawal after a work that moves me. The ones I remember most clearly are probably *Evangelion*, *Cyberpunk*, *Chainsaw Man*, and so on…

Perhaps this also explains my love of big AAA games. I do not play simply for the thrill; I am looking more for immersion and a sense of stepping away from reality.  
But am I really drawn to what each work offers? Or am I chasing that almost heart-stopping release when it ends?

…

Perhaps I do not need a definite answer. What I can say—and feel grateful for—is that I still seem able to empathize and lose myself in a story. I suspect that, inevitably, the enthusiasm and attention that let me enter these worlds will wear away as I grow older. So why worry about whether it is childish or silly? Before I become a dull adult, I sincerely hope to hold on to every rare chance to be moved.

After all, life has never burned as brightly as fictional worlds do.  
So, **“could it use a little touch of fantasy?”**
