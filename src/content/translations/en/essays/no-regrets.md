Strangely, this may be a feeling I ought to have had before the university entrance exam. Yet tonight it keeps circling in my mind: the startling realization that life is about to turn a page feels so clear and so strong.

Should I stay in China or go abroad? Honestly, I do not know. I have listened to friends and weighed the advantages and disadvantages from all sorts of angles. Everyone’s advice contradicts someone else’s; the AI analyses are a fog. After all that thinking, I still arrive at the thoroughly unhelpful conclusion that “there are pros and cons.”

In the end, I have decided to study abroad. I am deeply grateful that my family has given me the right to choose and the freedom to decide, but I truly cannot tell whether this is the best answer… I often joke that I have picked the hardest route: twelve years of exam-driven education in China, followed by a university abroad that is easy to enter and hard to graduate from. The road ahead is full of thorns. Can I pass Spanish B2 within a year? Can I find my place quickly in a completely different society? Can I get the kind of job I want and cover the high cost of living? With the international situation so complicated, is studying abroad really the better choice? I wish I could see through to the heart of things, instead of suffering later over a decision I regret.

Then why go abroad at all? Embarrassingly, the reason behind my decision seems rather willful: I want to experience different possibilities in life… We do not get to start over, and I truly want to see different worlds. There is so much uncertainty, but I am still willing to try. I do not want a life whose ending I can already see, or work that amounts to waiting out the days. Where this road leads is far from clear; ahead could be a breathtaking view or an abyss. But perhaps that is what makes a choice compelling. The uncertainty of the future gives us a reason to keep living. Each unknown decision carries us further into our fate. There is no perfectly safe, optimal answer; the choices along the way have made us who we are. So perhaps I can afford to be a little bolder. There is no inherently wrong choice.

Well, here we are. Time to sleep.  
The sun will rise again, and life will go on.  
I hope—no, I am certain—that this will be a choice without regret.
