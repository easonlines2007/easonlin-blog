**(Whose embarrassing high-school essay is this?)**

When desire reaches far beyond our abilities, we suffer from helplessness. When our abilities exceed our desires, we drift into the idle boredom of giving up and lying flat. We are all people of feeling and longing, setting out toward our ideals; we are also ordinary people, held back by the limits of our abilities. Desire keeps going; strength runs out. Perhaps only by finding a balance between the two can we make the most of what life means.

To want nothing is not necessarily to feel at ease. A life of few desires may bring freedom, but freedom does not always bring peace. Out on life’s ocean, losing the lighthouse of ambition might feel liberating. Yet one can spend a lifetime drifting, only to sink into that nameless, boundless water and disappear. How often do we meet the carefree hermits of poetry, pouring their own wine and singing through the wild wind? Most of us are unwilling to give everything up. Without desire there is no direction; without direction, little interest. Life draws meaning from striving. Raising our aspirations to meet our abilities can make it fuller.

Yet consumed by ambition, we struggle to find release. As the old line goes, the feudal lords had only so much land, while Qin’s appetite knew no end. Human desire has no natural stopping point. As it expands without limit, ability inevitably falls behind. We chase a mirage we cannot reach, and look down to find the ground at our feet still in ruins. The vast distance between reality and our ideals brings immense pain. Unless we reconsider, we may drown, like Jia Lian, in the damp shadows of our own fantasies. Extend this to society, and a world of endlessly frustrated appetites becomes dangerous. “Without indulging desire, everything loses its interest.” Yes, indulgence offers pleasure and illusion. But when our abilities cannot sustain it, the temptation is to break laws and rules. If everyone takes desperate risks to satisfy private wants, what stability can remain? Lowering our desires to fit our abilities may bring greater ease.

Someone may ask, “Why not improve your abilities, so that you can fulfill greater desires?” That is fair. Working toward a goal and growing through the effort is a healthy attitude. But ideals are generous; reality is less forgiving. Neither ability nor desire grows in a straight line. As we approach the limits of our own capacities, further gains become harder, while desire can expand exponentially. Without a grounded outlook, relentlessly charging at an endless series of chasms leaves us unsatisfied however hard we try, and brings imbalance and self-doubt. Clear-eyed, with a healthy mind, we must steady the scales of ambition and desire. That is the narrow gate worth seeking.

“A single thought in the human heart is known to heaven and earth.” After seeing all the splendor of the world, after letting go of our many attachments, have we understood how to balance strength and longing?

Steady those scales as they sway in the wind. This is the narrow gate to success; this is what can bear the weight of a life.
