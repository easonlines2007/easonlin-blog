Honestly, I should be asleep by now.

But after tossing and turning, I have concluded that sleep is not happening. Hardly surprising: after two or three weeks of going to bed at two in the morning, my body clock has made itself comfortable.

I stare at the familiar ceiling and try to empty my mind, only to find, absurdly, that it already seems empty.  
I close my eyes and try to force myself to sleep, only to discover, rather shamefully, that my head is full of useless scraps.

When will I break the habit of scrolling through short videos before bed?  
How long has it been since I had a vivid, complete dream that stayed with me long after waking?

Looking back, my mind seemed to work best when I was ten or a little older. Without any outside help, I could conjure vast fantastical scenes—so real, every detail alive.

Later, I became quite obsessed with big AAA games. My explanation then was that I was not playing to relax or for the thrill of it, but for immersion and escape into something entirely different from reality. Looking at it now, perhaps I was simply using increasingly lifelike digital worlds to make up for my fading imagination.

A sad thought: these days, I seem to be losing even the patience to settle down with a big game.

In an age of information overload, my head is crammed with irrelevant bits of junk. The first thing I do in the morning is reach for my phone to see what is trending today.

…

What the hell does any of it have to do with me? Am I supposed to watch a few current-affairs commentaries and suddenly hold forth like an expert? See a few personal-testimony videos and swing from one side to the other again? Why spend my energy wading through the cesspit of online arguments?

…

I do not know. Perhaps that was the point of writing this—I am finally getting sleepy.
