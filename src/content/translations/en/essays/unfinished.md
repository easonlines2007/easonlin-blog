Playing here and there in stolen pockets of time, I finally finished *Black Myth: Wukong*. As the credits rolled, I felt an aching sense of loss—the highest praise I can give a video game.

It also felt time to write something, to say goodbye to an old friend I first met about four years ago. After turning it over in my mind, “unfinished” still seemed the most fitting way to end.

The labels attached to *Black Myth*—“China’s first,” “taking Chinese culture overseas”—have long since become familiar refrains. Rather than repeat grand declarations, I would prefer to talk about something more personal.

Is this little black monkey perfect? Set aside the affection, and the answer is clearly no. For all its extraordinary visuals, art, music, and satisfying gameplay, so much remains unfinished. Invisible walls everywhere, almost no navigational guidance, maddening boss hitboxes: all reveal how inexperienced some of Game Science’s design decisions still are.

But why must I set that affection aside? It is not blind loyalty to a Chinese-made game. It is the natural, simple recognition of someone shaped by the same culture. That moving sense of familiarity is precious: the shiver when *Celestial Symphony* begins, the feeling of stepping into a distinctly Chinese artistic world. Yes, while playing, I got furious and lost my composure, but I also felt exhilarated. I cursed the developers for their devious tricks, yet I could feel their enthusiasm and sincerity. The good and the bad were woven together into a magnificent journey west.

What follows may sound affected, but allow me a little self-indulgent complaining.

It is easy to talk freely about a game; harder to ignore the mess of real life. The little monkey has brought quite a few things to an end. How many of my own remain unfinished? Months into my final year of high school, I still cannot shake my aimless habits. When will I change? I do not know. How will the university entrance exam go? I do not know that either. And what can I say about studying abroad after that? There are so many unknowns. When will I be ready to face them all? I suppose I still do not know.

Even so, I am willing to dream foolishly once more. I hope I will stop drifting through life like the half-alive person I am now. More than that, I hope that, walking toward each unfinished mountain in the distance, I can bear the grit grinding beneath my feet…

The little monkey’s journey west is over. Barring surprises, mine still has another seventy-odd years to run. But why worry about every last thing? You can never be completely ready, after all, and “setting out on the pilgrimage matters far more than reaching the sacred mountain.”

—A small attempt to glimpse the road ahead.
