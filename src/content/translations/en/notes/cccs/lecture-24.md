## Core Introduction and Concept Panorama

Analyze the wonderful collision between the Cold War military-industrial race and the wave of mass consumerism after World War II, and explore the commercialization history of microprocessors from top-secret military projects to civilian pocket calculators and electronic game consoles.

---

## 1. The huge nurturing of Cold War military industrial orders and the desire for civilian use

*(Reference time: 00:01:20 · [Video jump (P24 00:01:20)](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1EW411u7th/?p=24&t=80))*

Throughout the 1950s and 1960s, behind the rapid evolution of computer technology stood the invisible hands of the Cold War arms race:
- Intercontinental ballistic missiles require extremely small, shock-resistant and heat-resistant, highly reliable micro-avionics computers (such as Minuteman missiles and Apollo lunar spacecraft guidance computers AGC);
- The U.S. Department of Defense and NASA spent a lot of money, almost underwriting all the first-generation microchips produced by Silicon Valley's Fairchild and Texas Instruments at high prices regardless of cost.

However, although military industry orders solve the survival problem of start-up technologies, military technology is closed and strict and cannot form a virtuous cycle of large-scale markets. The real explosion of computer technology requires a commercial fire that ignites the mass civilian market.

## 2. Vannevar Bush’s Prophecy: Memex and the Personal Message Desk

*(Reference time: 00:02:40 · [Video jump (P24 00:02:40)](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1EW411u7th/?p=24&t=160))*

As early as 1945, when World War II was about to end, Vannevar Bush, President Roosevelt’s science adviser, published a masterpiece that has been handed down since then: “As We Think” (*As We May Think*).

Bush envisioned a  **Memex**  The future of personal desk equipment:
- The desktop is embedded with a translucent glass projection screen, which integrates a microfilm film library, a quick retrieval motor and a mechanical keyboard;
- Individuals can compress and archive all the books, papers, diaries and business letters they have read throughout their lives in it;
- **Associative index and hyperlink prototype**: When reading a book, users can "tie" paragraphs of another related document together on the screen to form an interdisciplinary associative path, and they can jump to the page immediately by pressing a button.
This prose deeply shocked and enlightened generations of geeks, including Douglas Engelbart and Tim Berners-Lee, and outlined a grand ultimate blueprint for hypertext networks and personal computers.

## 3. Pioneer of consumer electronics: Sony transistor radio and birth of microprocessor

*(Reference time: 00:04:10 · [Video jump (P24 00:04:10)](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1EW411u7th/?p=24&t=250))*

The first company to successfully transform advanced military semiconductor technology into mass consumer products was Japan. **Sony**.

In 1955, Sony founders Ibuka Dai and Morita Akio keenly seized the opportunity of American Western Electric Company (AT&T) to liberalize the transistor civilian patent licensing and developed mankind's first portable **Transistor Radio (TR-55)**: 
- The radio was originally a vacuum-tube behemoth that was as bulky as furniture in the living room, but Sony reduced it to something that could fit in a coat pocket;
- For the first time, young people around the world could leave their parents' living rooms and listen to rock music outdoors and on the beach anytime and anywhere. Millions of units were sold! This proves: **As long as high technology is miniaturized, portable, and consumerized, it can trigger a bottomless buying frenzy in the mass market.**

#### Intel 4004’s business-breaking opportunities
In 1969, the Japanese calculator manufacturer Busicom approached the newly established Intel and hoped to customize 12 special chips for their new desktop office calculator. Intel engineers Ted Hoff and Federico Faggin believed that making proprietary chips for each product was too wasteful and proposed: **Why not compress and integrate all the computing logic into a single general-purpose chip, and define its functions through software programming?**
 Therefore, mankind’s first general-purpose microprocessor——**Intel 4004**  was born! The arrival of microprocessors completely shattered the door to the exclusive privileges of mainframe computers.

## 4. Video game enlightenment and the snowball effect of consumerism

*(Reference time: 00:07:20 · [Video jump (P24 00:07:20)](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1EW411u7th/?p=24&t=440))*

After the cost of microprocessors plummeted, the first killer field to sweep through the masses was not serious office software, but **Video Games**!

In 1972, Nolan Bushnell founded Atari and launched the coin-operated arcade game that became a sensation in the United States——**"Pong" (table tennis)**: 
- The picture is extremely crude: two rackets made of white cursors on the left and right of the screen, with a white light spot in the middle acting as a ball bouncing around;
- The test machine was placed in a local bar in California. The next day, the coin box was completely jammed by the 25 cent coins poured in by the swarms of drinkers!

Game arcades and subsequent home video game consoles (such as the Atari 2600) created incredible business wealth. The huge profits fed back into chip fabs to expand production capacity and dilute R&D costs, ultimately preparing all the hardware ammunition for the personal computer (PC) revolution that broke out in the late 1970s.
